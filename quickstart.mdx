This guide walks you through creating a complete viewer project from scratch — from preparing your GLB file to sharing a live URL.

## Before you start

You need:
- A `.glb` file exported from Blender with at least one animated camera track
- Access to the Animator Dashboard (a valid account with editor or admin permissions on the project)

<Note>
  If your GLB does not yet have a camera animation, see [GLB requirements](/guides/glb-requirements) for the Blender export settings the pipeline expects.
</Note>

## Step 1 — Upload your model

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the GLB ingest dialog">
    In the dashboard header, click the **Upload Model** button to open the **Pipeline GLB Ingest** dialog.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select or drag your file">
    Drag a `.glb` file onto the drop zone, or click **Choose GLB file** to browse. The file must use the `.glb` extension (binary glTF format).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the validation report">
    The analyzer runs five pipeline checks in sequence:

    1. **Reading file** — verifies the file can be read as binary data
    2. **Validating binary** — checks the GLB header and chunk structure
    3. **Parsing scene** — loads the scene graph using the glTF runtime
    4. **Applying pipeline rules** — validates materials, textures, camera animations, and extensions
    5. **Validation complete** — issues a `pass` or `fail` result

    A `pass` result enables the **Load Into Viewer** button.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Load the model">
    Click **Load Into Viewer**. The model appears in the 3D viewport and the timeline editor becomes active.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Step 2 — Define timeline steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the timeline editor">
    The timeline editor appears at the bottom of the viewport. It shows the full frame range of your model's animation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a step">
    Click **Add Step** (or drag the step boundary handles) to mark a frame range. Each step needs:
    - A **name** displayed in the viewer
    - A **start frame** and **end frame** from the animation
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set an optional camera override">
    For each step you can pin a specific camera position and target so the viewer automatically focuses the right part of the model.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repeat for all steps">
    Add as many steps as needed. Steps are ordered by their `order` field — you can reorder them in the editor.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Keep step names short — they appear in the step controls bar at the bottom of the viewer. Use the **stepText** field for longer descriptions.
</Tip>

## Step 3 — Assign parts and tools

1. Open the **Parts** panel from the floating toolbar.
2. For each step, select the parts the user needs to have on hand.
3. Open the **Tools** panel and assign the required tools.

Parts and tools appear in side panels when the user navigates to that step in the live viewer.

## Step 4 — Control object visibility

1. Open the **Visibility** panel.
2. For each step, toggle individual mesh objects to **shown** or **hidden**.

Objects marked as hidden for a step are not rendered when the viewer is at that step, focusing the user's attention on the relevant components.

## Step 5 — Save your draft

Click **Save** in the toolbar. The dashboard sends your timeline steps, visibility assignments, tools, and parts to the server as a new draft revision.

<Note>
  Each save increments the draft revision number. You can browse revision history from the project settings panel.
</Note>

## Step 6 — Preview and deploy

<Steps>
  <Step title="Generate a preview">
    Click **Preview** in the publish controls. The dashboard builds a release from your current draft and returns a time-limited preview URL. Share this link with reviewers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the preview viewer">
    Open the preview URL to see the viewer exactly as end users will see it: step controls, parts panel, tools panel, and camera animations all active.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deploy to live">
    When the preview looks correct, click **Deploy Live**. The dashboard promotes the preview release to the live URL for your project.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Your live viewer URL follows the pattern:

```
/viewer/live/{companyId}/{productId}
```

Share this URL with end users. It serves the published manifest until you deploy a new release.
